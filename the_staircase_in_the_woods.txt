The Staircase in the Woods

There’s a hole in my memory, a black spot that I can’t seem to fill. It’s been ten years, but the dread is still there, a constant, low hum in the back of my mind. They say time heals all wounds, but they’re wrong. Some wounds fester. Some wounds never close.

It was one of those stupid things you do as a kid, you know? One of those “I dare you” moments that feels like the most important thing in the world at the time. There were four of us: me, Liam, Chloe, and Noah. We were all sixteen, bored out of our minds, and living in a town where the most exciting thing to do was to go cow-tipping. We were desperate for a story, something to make our summer memorable. We got one, but not the kind we wanted.

We’d heard the rumors, of course. Whispers of a staircase in the middle of Blackwood Forest. A staircase that led to nowhere. They said it was old, made of wrought iron, and just… there. No house, no ruins, nothing. Just a staircase. People said it was a place you shouldn’t go near, that strange things happened there. That people went missing. But we were sixteen, and we were invincible.

So, one sweltering July afternoon, we packed a cooler with cheap sodas and even cheaper beer and drove out to the edge of the woods in Liam’s beat-up pickup. The air was thick and humid, the kind of heavy that makes it hard to breathe. The woods were dense, and the sunlight barely trickled through the canopy, painting the forest floor in a gloomy, green-tinged light.

“You guys actually believe this crap?” Noah said, swatting at a mosquito. He was always the skeptic, the one who tried to act too cool for everything.

“It’s not about believing, it’s about the adventure,” Chloe shot back, her voice full of a nervous energy that we all felt. “Come on, Noah, where’s your sense of fun?”

We walked for what felt like hours, the initial excitement slowly draining away, replaced by a growing sense of unease. The woods were quiet. Too quiet. No birds, no rustling in the undergrowth. Just the sound of our own footsteps and our own ragged breathing. The silence was oppressive, like a physical weight on our shoulders.

And then we saw it.

It was just like the stories said. A wrought-iron staircase, spiraling upwards into the dense canopy of leaves. It was out of place, a bizarre, man-made object in the middle of untouched nature. It was rusted and covered in moss, but it looked… solid. Like it had been there forever.

We stood there for a long moment, just staring. The silence was deafening.

“No way,” Liam finally breathed, his voice a mixture of awe and fear. “It’s real.”

We circled it, like a pack of nervous animals. It didn't feel right. It was like a glitch in reality, a piece of a puzzle that didn't fit. The air around it felt colder, heavier. I could feel the hairs on my arms stand up.

“I’m going up,” Chloe announced, her voice trembling slightly.

“Are you crazy?” I said, grabbing her arm. “We don’t know what that is.”

“It’s a staircase, you idiot,” she said, pulling away. “What’s the worst that could happen?” She tried to sound brave, but I could see the fear in her eyes.

She started to climb. Each step creaked under her weight, the sound echoing through the silent woods. Liam and Noah followed her, their faces a mixture of excitement and apprehension. I was the only one who stayed at the bottom, a knot of dread tightening in my stomach. I had a bad feeling about this, a really bad feeling.

“Come on, you wimp!” Noah called down, his voice sounding strange and distant. “Don’t be a baby.”

I shook my head, my feet feeling like they were rooted to the ground. I couldn’t explain it, but I felt a primal fear, a voice in the back of my head screaming at me to run.

They reached the top, their forms disappearing into the thick leaves. I could hear their muffled laughter, the sound tinny and far away.

“There’s nothing up here!” Chloe shouted, her voice sounding… different. Faint. Like it was coming from a great distance.

And then, the screaming started.

It was a sound I’ll never forget. A raw, guttural scream of pure terror. It was Chloe. Then Liam. Then Noah. Their screams cut through the silence of the woods, one after another, and then… nothing.

Just silence.

I stood there, frozen, my heart hammering against my ribs. I called out their names, my voice cracking with fear. No answer. I waited for what felt like an eternity, my mind racing through a thousand different horrific scenarios. I thought I heard a whisper on the wind, a faint, chilling sound that I couldn’t quite make out.

Finally, I did the only thing I could think of. I ran. I ran as fast as I could, not looking back, the branches and thorns tearing at my skin. I didn't stop until I burst out of the woods and back onto the dirt road where we had parked the truck.

I called the police, my voice a hysterical mess. They searched for days. They brought in dogs, helicopters, volunteers. They found nothing. No bodies, no signs of a struggle, nothing. They didn’t even find the staircase. It was like it had never been there.

They questioned me for hours, their voices a mixture of suspicion and pity. I told them what happened, but I could see the disbelief in their eyes. A staircase in the middle of the woods? It sounded insane. They thought I was on drugs, that I was covering for something. Eventually, they let me go, but I could feel their eyes on me for months afterward.

It’s been ten years since that day. I moved away from that town, tried to start a new life. But I can’t escape it. I still have nightmares. I see their faces, hear their screams. And sometimes, when I’m driving on a lonely road at night, I think I see it. A flash of wrought iron in the woods, a staircase that leads to nowhere. And I know, deep in my bones, that they’re still there. Somewhere. Waiting. And I wonder, if I had climbed those stairs with them, would I have disappeared too? Or would I have found something worse?
